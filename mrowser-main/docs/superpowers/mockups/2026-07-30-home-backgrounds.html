The gradient cycles
two questions before it ships

Same flat two-stop gradient as today. Instead of pick(seed) choosing one and holding it forever, the home screen starts on one palette and lerps its two colours toward the next, forever. In the app that is one GradientDrawable and one ValueAnimator — no extra layers, no overdraw. The stacked-and-crossfaded layers below are just how a browser fakes it.

Click a panel to fill the screen. Esc closes.

Start at 20× to see the cycle, confirm at 1×

Question one — are the palettes far enough apart?

Both cycle all six, 20s per step

Question two — what cadence?

All using whichever palette set wins above

Why the widened set exists. The six shipped palettes sit within about 6% of each other on every channel — #1A0E12 against #0E1A22 is a 16/255 difference at most. Spread over a 20-second fade that is below the perceptual floor: the animator runs, the screen looks static. Widening only touches the bottom stop; the top stays near-black so text at the top of the screen keeps its contrast.

Cost. Two ArgbEvaluator-style lerps and a GradientDrawable.setColors() per tick. At 10fps that is nothing — the shader is rebuilt, not recomposited, and there is exactly one full-screen layer either way. This is the cheapest motion of anything considered.

One requirement regardless. The animator has to stop when the home overlay is hidden. HomeView just goes GONE while you browse or watch, so an animator left running would keep ticking behind the WebView and through playback. Start in show(), stop in hide().
